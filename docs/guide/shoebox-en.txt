YOUR PHOTO LIBRARY, ON THE DRIVE.
=================================

shoebox is a small program that lives on your external drive next to your
photos. It indexes them, shows them in a fast timeline in your browser, and
helps you tag, sort and de-duplicate, without ever copying them or touching
their metadata.

- Originals stay untouched
- Lives on your drive
- Private: no cloud, no account
- Browse from the iPad too
- macOS today · Linux & Windows next

[Screenshot: The timeline: months, folders, search, type filter and year
jump.]

What shoebox can do
-------------------

One page in your browser, a timeline of everything on the drive.

- Timeline
   All photos and videos by month, with a year jump, folder tree and a viewer
   with full-size info.

- Search & filter
   Type a person, pet, tag or folder. Combine keywords for a more complex
   search. Filter by photos, videos, Live Photos or screenshots, or show only
   your favorites ♥.

- Tags
   Your own tags on many photos at once. Folders count as tags too. Stored in
   the index, never in the photos.

- Move & organise
   Move photos to a folder (created if needed). RAW, Live Photo and sidecar
   files travel along.

- Adding photos
   Copy the photos of an event onto the drive in Finder or File Explorer
   (dates are kept) and Scan. Copies of files the drive already had are
   listed, and you can delete them.

- Duplicates
   Finds identical, resized, edited and look-alike photos. You review; the
   best copy always stays.

- Safe trash
   Deleted photos go to a trash on the drive. Put them back, or delete them
   for good.

- People & pets
   Optional recognition groups faces, cats and dogs. You name them; shoebox
   suggests the rest.

- Several drives
   Browse the photos of several drives in one common timeline, even when they
   sit on different disks. Backups are recognised and checked.

- Locations
   Optional maps, off by default: a map in the info of photos with GPS data, a
   world map of all your photos, and named places like “Home” that collect
   their photos by themselves.

The screen at a glance
----------------------

Everything is on one page. Here is where things are.

1. Folders menu
   ☰ shows or hides the sidebar on narrow screens.

2. Search box
   People, pets, tags and folders; pick several to combine them.

3. Favorites ♥
   Show only the photos you have hearted. Also found by typing favorite in the
   search box.

4. Type
   Tick what to show; nothing ticked shows everything. Photos: still pictures
   without screenshots (hover the ⓘ). Videos: stand-alone videos. Live Photos:
   photos that come with a short clip. Screenshots: pictures of a phone or
   computer screen.

5. Year
   Jump straight to a year.

6. Select ☑
   Pick photos to move, tag or trash.

7. Quick links
   All photos · Duplicates · Trash · Settings.

8. Folders
   Your folders, with counts. Tags, Faces and Drives appear here when you have
   them, and so does Locations when maps are on.

9. Timeline
   Months with counts. Click a photo to open the viewer.

10. Language
   Switch English / German.

11. Status
   Scan, thumbnail and drive status at the bottom of the sidebar.

A typical session
Plug in the drive and double-click Start shoebox. Scan (quick when little has
changed) and open the photo app. To add the photos of an event, copy their
folder onto the drive in Finder (named like 2025-08 Holiday) and Scan again:
if some files were on the drive already, the launcher offers to delete the new
copies. Then tag and sort. Look at Duplicates now and then, and run the Backup
check after every backup.

Get started in three steps
--------------------------

macOS, Intel (10.13 or newer) and Apple Silicon. Nothing to install.

1. Put it on the drive
   Unpack shoebox-macos.tar.gz from the release. Copy shoebox-macos and Start
   shoebox.command to the top folder of your photo drive (next to your photo
   folders). Optional: the recognizer folder for faces. The archive also holds
   this guide in the folder guide.

2. Start the launcher
   Double-click Start shoebox.command. A Terminal window opens (leave it open)
   and the launcher appears in your browser. If macOS asks, right-click the
   file and choose Open.

3. Scan, then open
   Add your drive (click it, or type /Volumes/MyDrive), press Scan, then Start
   photo app. The first scan reads every file once and takes a while on a big
   drive; later scans only look at what changed. When it finishes, the tabs
   Moved, Changed, Missing and Already on the drive (each with its count) list
   exactly which files they mean; for new files you already had, the tab shows
   the older copy right next to each one.

Launcher tasks
Scan: find new, moved and changed photos. Fast: it only looks at what changed
since the last scan, and the tabs list what this scan changed. Run it after
you move, rename or add photos. It also lists new copies of files the drive
already had.
Verify: check that nothing was lost or damaged. It re-reads every file and
compares it with what the index remembers, which finds files that were
silently damaged on the drive. Slow, so run it now and then (every few months,
or before you rely on a backup), not after every move. Moves are not its
business: a moved photo has its new place in the index and passes. A photo
whose path is gone but whose content is still on the drive elsewhere (a
duplicate you deleted) is listed as "relocated", not as a failure. Only
content that is nowhere on the drive any more counts as missing. If files are
gone, a button appears after the check (and after a scan) that forgets their
records: they are removed from the shoebox index, its database on the drive.
No photo file is touched.
Recognize: find faces (and, with Recognize pets, cats and dogs)
Recognize lying down: also find faces lying down (slower)
Recognize pets: find cats and dogs; they show up among the faces
Face stats: how recognition went
Backup check: is the 2nd folder a complete copy of the 1st?

the photo app.]

Good to know
- Videos get preview images if you put a static ffmpeg next to the program
  (.shoebox/bin/ffmpeg). Without it, the browser grabs a frame instead.
- shoebox keeps its index and thumbnails in a hidden .shoebox folder on the
  drive, so the drive can move to another computer.
- Closing the Terminal window stops shoebox.

The app speaks English and German: pick the language at the bottom of the
sidebar.

Browse and view
---------------

Scroll through the timeline, jump by year, open a photo.

Timeline
Photos are grouped by capture date, newest first. The Year menu jumps; the
month heading shows where you are. A photo without a capture date takes the
month in the name of its event folder (2020-07, 2020.07, 20-07 or 20.07), or
else the earlier of the file’s created and modified dates; the info panel then
marks the date as estimated.

Folders
The sidebar mirrors the folders on your drive. Click one to see only its
photos. Rename or move a folder from its chip.

Viewer
Click a photo. ⓘ opens the info panel: date (marked estimated when it is not
the day the photo was taken), camera, size, tags, people, and buttons to open
the folder or copy the path. At the bottom, GPS data shows where the photo was
taken (see “Locations and maps”).

Favorites
The ♥ in the top bar of the viewer, or the key f, marks the photo as a
favorite. In the timeline the same heart sits in the top right corner of each
photo (it appears when you point at a photo; hearted photos always show it).
Click it again to take it off. Under the hood a favorite is just your own tag
favorite, kept in the index like any tag, never in the photo.

Rotate
r turns left, Shift+R right. For JPEGs this changes only the two bytes of the
rotation flag; for other formats the turn exists in shoebox only.

[Screenshot: The viewer with its info panel.]

Keyboard in the viewer
← →: previous / next
i: show / hide the info panel
Space: open / close the viewer (on a photo picked with the arrow keys)
f: add to / remove from favorites
r · Shift+R: rotate left / right
Esc: close the viewer

Find and organise
-----------------

Search, select, tag and move: all of it works on many photos at once.

Search
Type in the search box: suggestions appear for tags, people and folders. Pick
several and they become chips that narrow the result; × removes one, Clear all
starts over. To find your favorites press the ♥ button next to Type, or type
favorite (English) or favorit (German) and pick ♥ Favorites; it combines with
everything else, e.g. favorites of one person or one year. With maps on (see
“Locations and maps”) the suggestions also offer your places.

Type offers Photos (still pictures without screenshots), Videos, Live Photos
and Screenshots; tick any of them, and nothing ticked shows everything. To
leave screenshots out of the timeline, tick Photos, Videos and Live Photos but
not Screenshots; to see only screenshots, tick just Screenshots. Shoebox
recognises a screenshot from the file (a PNG without camera data, a name like
Screenshot or Bildschirmfoto, the size of a phone or computer screen) and from
the picture itself (large areas of one colour). It can be wrong in both
directions: in the photo’s info panel, Screenshot is a drop-down that starts
on shoebox’s guess; pick Yes or No to correct it, and Back to automatic
returns to the guess. Your choice is kept in the library; the photo file is
never changed.

Select
Press ☑, then click photos (Shift-click for a range; on the iPad just tap).
“Select all” on a month heading takes the whole month.

Tag
Add tag… puts your own tag on the selection. Tags live in shoebox’s index,
never in the photo files.

Move
Move… asks for a folder. New folders are created, nothing is ever overwritten,
and RAW, Live Photo and sidecar files move along. The photos’ own tags always
go with them. “Keep folder tags” also keeps the old folder’s tags as own tags;
folder tags always follow the new folder.

Adding photos, duplicates and trash
-----------------------------------

Adding photos
Copy or move the photos onto the drive in Finder or File Explorer, which keeps
their dates, ideally into a folder named YYYY-MM Event, and press Scan. If the
scan finds new files the drive already had, the launcher marks them already on
the drive and offers Delete the new copies (to the trash, or for good). The
older original stays and gets the deleted copy’s folder name as a tag.
Settings sets how event folders are named in suggestions (for example
YY.MM_Event); existing folders are not renamed and all forms are recognised.

Duplicates
Duplicates lists four kinds: identical, same photo in another size, original
and edited, and look-alikes. Tick delete this copy on what you want gone; the
best file stays and at least one per group always does. Tags and capture dates
of a removed copy carry over.

Shortcuts
Clear Same Folder Copies and Clear Lower Quality Copies clean up the clear-cut
cases in one go.

What to do with a group
Every group has up to two buttons at its top right. Keep all means: these
files stay, and they belong together. Different photos (only on groups of
look-alikes) means: these are separate shots, not copies of each other. Either
way nothing is deleted and no photo is touched; shoebox only makes a note in
its own database on the drive, so it never offers this group again. The note
follows the photos if you move or rename them. Keep all also lists the others
as Versions in the info panel of each photo. Photos you have decided about are
also left alone by Clear Same Folder Copies and Clear Lower Quality Copies. To
delete instead, tick delete this copy, or open a look-alike photo, compare
them large and press Keep this one: after you confirm, all the others go to
the trash.

Trash
Nothing is deleted right away. Trashed photos wait in .shoebox/trash on the
drive: Put back or Delete for good. Moving photos to the trash is off by
default: switch it on once in Settings (Allow move to trash).

People and pets
---------------

Optional: shoebox finds faces, cats and dogs; you give them names.

Find them
Install the recognizer once (recognizer/install.sh /Volumes/MyDrive). Then
close the photo app and run Recognize (and Recognize pets) in the launcher. A
Faces section appears in the sidebar.

Name them
Under Unnamed, shoebox shows cards of similar faces. Type a name once and the
whole card is named; later, confirm (✓) or reject (✗) the suggestions. The
next two pages show every step, with groups and the buttons for strangers and
false finds.

In the viewer
The info panel lists who is on the photo and draws a box around each face or
pet. Click a name to see all their photos, or draw a box around a face or pet
that was missed.

Search
Names work in the search box like tags, and so do kinds such as “all cats”.
Combine a person with a tag, a folder or another person.

New photos
Recognition does not start by itself after a scan yet. Close the photo app and
run Recognize again: it only looks at the new photos.

Tag faces, step by step
-----------------------

After Recognize, every face waits for a name. You do not have to name them
all.

1. Open Unnamed
   In the sidebar, open Faces and then Unnamed. Each card holds faces that
   look alike, the biggest card first. Cats and dogs have cards of their own;
   the menu above the cards chooses People, Pets or both.

2. Name a card
   Type the name once and press Name: the whole card is named. Start typing to
   pick a person you already have (on a card of pets only cats and dogs are
   offered, and the other way round), or choose + New person (on a card of
   pets it reads + New cat, + New dog or + New pet). After that shoebox
   suggests more faces of them (Looks like Mia): confirm with ✓ or reject with
   ✗.

3. Strangers and false finds
   Ignore is for strangers, and for faces on posters, screens and pictures:
   nobody needs to name them. Not a face (for pets Not a pet) is for false
   finds that are no face at all. Nothing is deleted and no photo is touched;
   shoebox only notes the decision in its own database.

4. Only some faces of a card
   Select lets you tick the faces yourself. The buttons then work on those
   only: Name 2, Ignore 2, 2 not a face. Use it when a card mixes two people.

Several faces in a photo, and groups
------------------------------------

Tick faces in the viewer. Sort people into groups such as Family, Friends and
Colleagues.

In a photo
Open a photo and its info panel (ⓘ). Under People every face has its picture
and a name, or + Name. Select turns the list into a checklist: tick several
faces (All unnamed ticks every face nobody has named), then Ignore or Not a
face for all of them at once. + Add face or pet draws a box around a face or
pet that was missed. Say what it is first, Person, Cat or Dog, then name it:
the list offers only names of that kind (no people when you name a cat), and +
New person, + New cat or + New dog adds a new one. The kind of a drawn pet
cannot be changed later; delete the box and draw it again.

Groups
Faces shows people by group. New group… makes one; Groups… renames, reorders
and deletes groups; to reorder, drag a row by the dotted handle on its left
(or focus the handle and press Alt+↑/↓). A person is in one group at most, and
deleting a group deletes nobody.

Put people into groups
Use ⋯ on a person, then Move to group…, or drag the person onto a group in the
sidebar. Pets work the same way: a group Pets can hold the dog and the cat.

Corrections
The same ⋯ menu has Merge into… for two cards of one person. On a person’s
page, Review faces lists Confirmed, Suggested, Maybe and Not them, and a face
marked by mistake can go back with ↺. Calibration (Face check, Pet check)
shows what recognition found: small faces, scores and false finds.

Locations and maps
------------------

Optional: where your photos were taken, on a map. Off until you turn it on.

Turn maps on
Open Settings and tick Show maps (OpenStreetMap). The maps are drawn in your
browser from OpenStreetMap, so this is the one place where shoebox needs the
internet: OpenStreetMap sees which parts of the map you look at, never your
photos or their names. shoebox saves no map pictures on the drive (your
browser may keep some in its own cache). With the box unticked nothing is ever
loaded from the internet.

In a photo’s info
Where the file has GPS data, GPS data in the info panel shows the position
and, with maps on, a small square map with a pin on it, fixed to the bottom of
the panel. Without internet the map says Offline instead. A photo without GPS
data shows no map at all, only the heading with a Set location… button.

Give a photo a location
Set location… (right next to the heading GPS data) opens a dialog with a map
and two fields, latitude and longitude, which are always both there and follow
each other: click the map to place the pin, or type the numbers (a point or a
comma both work; latitude −90 to 90, longitude −180 to 180, anything else is
refused). With maps off, only the fields work. The location is saved in
shoebox only: the photo file is not changed, and it stays with the photo if
you move it. A location you gave can be changed or removed again; a location
that is in the file itself is never changed.

The Locations page
With maps on, Locations appears in the sidebar below Folders. It opens a world
map with a pin for every photo that has a location; pins close together are
grouped into a number and split apart as you zoom in. Click a pin or a group
and the screen splits: the map on the left, the photos from there on the
right, like the timeline. Click a photo to view it; the arrow at the top of
the list folds it away.

Places
A place is a named area, like “Home” or “Island XY”. Press New place, click
two opposite corners on the map and give it a name: every photo with a
location inside the area belongs to it, now and later. Places are listed under
Locations in the sidebar with their number of photos; click one to see its
photos. You can also search for a place by name. If the search is empty,
picking a place opens it on the map, like a click in the sidebar (a Type
filter is dropped then, as on every click in the sidebar). If the search
already has chips (a folder, tag, person, pet, favorites ♥), the place joins
them as a chip 📍 and you stay in the timeline: all chips must match, so “Home”
+ “Anna” shows Anna’s photos taken at home. A second place replaces the first.
The 🗺 on the chip shows the place on the map, and a place chosen on the map
goes along when you add another chip. In the list on the right you can Rename
a place, Draw area again or delete it; only the place goes, never a photo or
its location.

Places and the locations you set are written to userdata.json next to the
index on the drive, together with your tags and people.

Drives, backups and the iPad
----------------------------

Several drives
Start shoebox with more than one drive and each one appears in the sidebar.
All drives gives you one common timeline across all of them, and shows
backups, photos that exist on more than one drive, and people across drives. A
drive that is unplugged is shown as offline.

Backup check
Choose the original first and the backup second. shoebox compares the two
indexes (no photo is read) and lists what is not on the backup yet, what
differs and what exists only on the backup. Also re-read the backup finds bit
rot.

iPad & other devices
In Terminal: shoebox serve /Volumes/MyDrive --lan. It prints an address and a
PIN; open the address in Safari on the same Wi-Fi and enter the PIN once.
Swipe in the viewer, tap to select.

Command line (optional)
shoebox scan <folder>: index new, moved and changed files
shoebox verify <folder>: re-read files and compare with the index
shoebox recognize <folder> [--pets]: find faces (and pets)
shoebox backup <original> <backup>: is the backup complete?
shoebox serve <folder> [--lan] [--pin …]: open the photo app

Your photos are safe
--------------------

The design rule is simple: shoebox reads your originals and never writes
anything into them.

- Read-only
   Metadata, thumbnails, tags and locations you set are kept in the index on
   the drive, never inside a photo. Capture dates and created dates never
   change.

- Guarded
   Every scan is checked: size, dates and a full hash before and after. Any
   difference is an error.

- Only on your command
   Originals change only through your actions: move, rename, trash. Always a
   rename, never a copy, and only if the file still matches the index. The
   only other thing shoebox removes is a new copy of a file the drive already
   had, and only when you press the button after a scan.

- Local
   No cloud, no account. By default only this computer can connect; other
   devices need --lan and a PIN. The one exception is the optional maps
   (Settings, off by default): your browser then loads map pictures from
   OpenStreetMap.

If something looks off
----------------------

The grid is empty. Run Scan in the launcher first, then start the photo app.

New photos are missing. Scan again. It is quick: only new and changed files
are read.

A video has no preview. Add ffmpeg (see “Good to know”) and restart the photo
app.

The iPad cannot connect. Same Wi-Fi? Started with --lan? Enter the PIN printed
in Terminal. Restarting shoebox asks for the PIN again.

A drive shows as offline. Plug it in again; shoebox picks it up when you
reload.

A map is empty or says Offline. Maps need the internet. Check the connection
and open the photo again; without it everything else works as before.
